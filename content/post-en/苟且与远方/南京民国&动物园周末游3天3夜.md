By the standards of tourist cities, Nanjing's content is extraordinarily rich. Before we left I asked my wife: Nanjing can be played many ways — the Six Dynasties (mostly before the Sui and Tang), the Ming, the Republican era, the War of Resistance, the zoo, the Yangtze, natural scenery, and a great many museums. What do you want to see? She said the Republican era. Then, thinking of our daughter, we added the top-tier Hongshan Forest Zoo and cut museums down to a minimum. The goal was clear; the trip happened; we set off.

## Day 1: Hongshan Forest Zoo

I first learned of this zoo from Hua Shi's book Visiting Zoos Is Serious Business, which systematically lays out the character, the pros and cons, and the essential mission of zoos around the world. A good zoo is a window onto how animals actually live and can even let you feel the beauty of nature and reconsider the human relationship with it. Entertainment, though, has never been a great zoo's first priority.

Hongshan is China's representative of that approach: it puts animal welfare first and provides conditions as close to the wild as it can. Visitors' wish to look gives way to the animals' own survival. You may find it hard to take good photos here, but the animals' condition and the presentation will delight you.

![Illustrated Hongshan guide map marking the four blocks: Dahong Hill, Xiaohong Hill, Fangniu Hill and the South Gate New Area.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/f99e2f5210e3823d14cdda65913bf353.webp)

Hongshan is divided into four blocks: Dahong Hill to the north, Xiaohong Hill to the northwest, Fangniu Hill to the southeast, and the South Gate New Area to the southwest.

The route I had planned was this: see all of the South Gate New Area, then go to the food plaza on Fangniu Hill for lunch. Walk south along the south side of Fangniu Hill, pass Gondwanaland and reach the Hippo House. From the Hippo House take the shuttle to the Elephant House stop, then tour the north side of Fangniu Hill — Gaoligong, the Koala House and so on. Walk to the felid cluster on the northeast side of Dahong Hill, skip the amusement area, and turn back to the New Tiger Reserve. Then follow the south side of Dahong Hill to the native animal area in the southwest, taking in the Xiaohong Hill area and the Panda House along the way, and leave by the North Gate.

Walking it for real, I found the route completely impractical. The zoo is not large by Chinese standards, but because it is built on natural hillsides, the amount of information per square metre is astonishing — you cannot get through it in a day, not even a third of it. My advice for Hongshan: give up any ambition of seeing every animal in one day and pick just two or three target areas. Even if you did cover it all, the quality of the visit would fall back to that of an ordinary second- or third-tier city zoo, and you would miss what makes Hongshan special.

### South Gate New Area

![Wooden signpost whose four arrow boards point to Ode to Africa, African Village, Mini Dune and Forest of Wisdom.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/bad68041e3d691227dcd1a4c308d1733.webp)

Entering from the South Gate New Area, the first sign we saw was distinctive: its illustration hints at what the enclosure holds, and once you get it, it makes you smile. That is the enemy of cost, and it is also the zoo's sincerity.

![A giraffe stands by the trees while three zebras graze on dry ground, tall grass filling the foreground.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/b42a69c4b1d0ac35496736c9a735d594.webp)

Inside is a mixed enclosure for giraffes and zebras. It is separated from visitors by several metres of grass and a water channel, ruling out all interaction. The zoo's intention is clear, but there was a side benefit I had not expected: it also kills any urge to greet the animals. All afternoon, young and old, I heard no shouting, clapping or whistling to get an animal's attention.

And this seemingly simple arrangement shows Hongshan's thoughtfulness. The giraffes' food hangs in buckets high in the trees so that they feed in a fairly natural posture — I have not seen such a setup in most other zoos. Giraffes are tall and evolved an extremely efficient cooling system to keep their brains from overheating in the sparsely shaded savanna. Hongshan put up a shade canopy; the giraffe hides under it, which reduces the load on its cooling system.

![A framed sign titled The Order of Coexistence explains how giraffes, zebras, wildebeest and ostriches feed at different heights.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/e97aa7304a9b5cde7131e4c0d5eba278.webp)

The panel beside it does not follow the standard catalogue of kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species, predators and habits. It focuses on how giraffes, zebras, wildebeest and ostriches evolved different diets to share one habitat. The last time I saw this point made was at the Anji branch of the Zhejiang Museum of Natural History, not at any zoo.

![A long line of schoolchildren with backpacks walks the zoo path, a few adults among them and a blue placard raised ahead.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/787902ed98dd738c622eb45b2c47f116.webp)

On an ordinary weekday you can dodge the tourists but not the local primary-school autumn outings. Yet because everyone is simply observing, with zero interaction with the animals, people move through quickly and you do not wait long for a viewing spot.

![A pale fennec fox lies curled on the sand, eyes shut, its oversized ears splayed beside two flat stone slabs.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/73626e997cd6429cab21d6cd5bf79989.webp)

The fennec fox enclosure — the smallest canid in the world. Small head, big ears; visitors coo over how cute it is.

Most features on an animal have a survival purpose. Fennec foxes live in deserts and hunt rodents and insects that hide under the sand, where keen hearing beats eyesight. And because desert days are scorching, their big ears carry a rich blood supply that gives them extreme cooling power.

Nanjing's climate, though, is not the desert, and it does get cold. Hongshan hung high-power lamps above the stone slabs, and far off behind the leaves three fennec foxes were huddled together on a slab warmed by the lamps.

![Two New Year-print posters of armed fennec foxes on a wooden gate warn against tapping the glass and feeding, a red no-entry sign between them.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/bd1ce18fad577b2aa5e3c5b3487ac853.webp)

Another small design that made me smile. This is the staff entrance beside the fennec fox enclosure, where the two visitor rules the zoo repeats everywhere have been turned into animal door gods. You can imagine the organisation behind this zoo, these people and the way they work — without this kind of love, such creations would never be brought to the front.

![Two meerkats stand alert on an expanse of bare sand scattered with logs, a stump and freshly dug burrow holes.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/852e2f126c5d77e21e0e0adc94540799.webp)

Next is the meerkat enclosure — for an animal of this size, it really is enormous. The patch in the photo is maybe a quarter of its area. Meerkats live in underground burrows, and the usual open, nothing-hidden display would make them lose natural behaviours. Even a pet shop keeping hamsters has to pile up wood shavings for them to burrow into; the same logic applies to meerkats.

![A sandy slope pocked with burrow entrances beside hollow logs, one hollowed stump opening as an exit, the glass barrier in the foreground.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/a3c0814c632c0b921cabb53dd3e1c2fc.webp)

A meerkat's burrow network can go two metres deep with a dozen entrances. Allowing them to dig freely means the substrate here is deep enough. Besides burrows, Hongshan thoughtfully placed hollow logs to give them refuges while they are above ground.

It is perfectly safe in a zoo, but they need it.

![A grey-headed Senegal parrot with a green back and orange belly perches on a sawn branch against a rough stone wall.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/f2e8643904050a3e5ce9cb6573acb978.webp)

The next enclosure holds African parrots, including a Senegal parrot from West Africa — fairly large, with grey-black feathers on its head, easy to identify.

![Three green Fischer's lovebirds with orange-red faces stand on straw-strewn ground beside a weathered log.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/1d19471e605715cedef62caa80e1c3f0.webp)

All the smaller ones in the enclosure are Fischer's lovebirds from East Africa, a lifelong-pairing parrot of the savanna.

The pile of dry grass on the ground is deliberate — you can see the bird on the right with a blade in its beak. I watched this window for a long time; these Fischer's lovebirds were showing natural nest-building behaviour. One by one they pick up dry grass in the beak or grab it with a foot and fly to some unseen corner. In Fischer's lovebirds the nest building is done mainly by the female, so these busy ones are females.

![Lovebirds perch on bare branches against a textured mud wall, one standing among the dry bamboo litter at lower right.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/6bc3bcf5b5abfd478e3a7769389249cc.webp)

Stepping back for this shot: the whole African parrot enclosure is this small, yet you can see the enrichment leaves few traces of human handiwork. For a small-bird enclosure it is a challenge to provide real plants — there is not enough sun and trees will not survive here. Look closely and the tree inside has no foliage of its own; the bamboo leaves are tied on. The most thoughtful touch is a branch reaching in from the upper right of the photo, lashed to that tree, with the binding also disguised in bamboo leaves. It forms something like a clothesline, and the parrots line up along it.

![An eastern black-and-white colobus rests in a tree fork, its white mantle draped over a black body and long white tail hanging below.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/5e1d5182883a8b05a0724ab81ed83853.webp)

We then entered a large combined primate area. This is the eastern black-and-white colobus, the star of the colobus genus. Its features are unmistakable: a tail like a black-and-white reversed brush, white fringe-like fur drifting like an immortal's robes, and a head that looks like it is wearing a padded northeastern winter hat.

![A colobus grips the trunk while feeding among leafy branches, a white brow band across its face, a companion's white mantle at right.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/26df2ea674b86dfea40fe4b7ebd344a9.webp)

Thinking back to the giraffe enclosure at the entrance, a pattern emerges. When Hongshan feeds animals inside an enclosure it tends to respect how they naturally live. These are arboreal animals that rarely come down, so the food is tied up in mid-air.

Colobus monkeys are named for their vestigial thumbs, reduced — in appearance and in bone — to a small nodule. That is not a disadvantage but an evolutionary choice. With no thumb in the way, the remaining fingers work like hooks, letting them move fast through the branches.

While this one was resting, a bee kept circling it — perhaps its white fur stands out too much in this setting. After several failed attempts to drive it off, the colobus, thoroughly annoyed, simply slipped away.

![A bamboo-weave board of handwritten cards and photos traces the African grey parrot's trade and protection year by year.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/2e490b92de10580495df57f7750e3170.webp)

Beside it is another large open-air parrot enclosure, for African grey parrots. One exhibit explains in detail the threats the population faces and the efforts made to protect the species.

![An African grey parrot perches on a log, its grey scalloped plumage set off by a bare white eye patch and a glimpse of red tail.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/45fa422b46655ece6f56e80f4990397d.webp)

This is a medium-to-large parrot, only slightly smaller than the macaws you see in many zoos. Their intelligence is top-tier among parrots, comparable to that of a four- to seven-year-old child.

![Yellow-green fern fronds are painted across the aviary's glass pane, a bare branch running below the timber frame.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/71e34bd7e75f7be4c2a7a108fef38b54.webp)

Rainforest leaves are painted on the glass on one side of the aviary to stop birds from flying into it and injuring themselves.

![A hand-painted board propped against bamboo shows a mandrill's face and warns visitors to watch quietly and not to feed or tease.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/fe2d8cad34f0598f3f231fd880d3ec4c.webp)

The mandrill enclosure is very large, with viewing openings on several sides, though the animals did not appear in our field of view.

The mandrill is the largest monkey species, with a face marked by a signature painted mask. They spend more time on the ground, live in groups, and are aggressive enough that even big cats think twice about provoking them.

![A dark green sign uses a plan of the indoor and outdoor zones to explain where the gorillas may be, listing times they retreat inside.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/4df0a5ec46a5c3f7e0ca8840c8f135ee.webp)

Likewise gorillas, being enormous, need a much larger range. They can move freely between indoor and outdoor areas and wander unpredictably; we did not see them either.

![Ochre cave-art figures - a leaping deer, smaller animals and an archer - are painted on an artificial rock wall, spiral motifs further along.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/5314199f39d3972042e85e9fa8c399d5.webp)

The indoor part of the gorilla house has a rather ceremonial artificial cave, its rock walls painted with murals in the style of our human ancestors.

![A patas monkey stands upright on a log facing the camera, chestnut back, pale limbs and white whiskers, its long tail trailing.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/dbc0a3201a506711a157125cc7cfc72e.webp)

The patas monkey is a ground-dwelling monkey that can run at about 50 km/h — the sprint champion of the primates. Look at their feet: they have evolved a flattened shape suited to running, rather like a cat's or dog's paw. Their limbs are long and slender, and the front and hind limbs are equal in length. Habitat and way of life together produced a species' unique features.

A female patas monkey is about half the size of a male. A few smaller females had been foraging in front of the glass; as soon as this one arrived they scattered.

![A small patas monkey crouches over fallen leaves, pawing through the litter with pale forepaws for food.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/300dde631fd8e5d7d32188a03875be80.webp)

Unlike the colobus, their food is not found in the trees. They keep rummaging on the ground, then pick up a pod-like plant from the leaf litter, crack the shell and eat the seeds inside.

![A carved lintel lettered Great Ape House tops the doorway, tattered red couplets on both pillars, visitors filing in through the glass doors.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/b620779ec6479123eccf35daf1aa8bf1.webp)

The outdoor section ends here, and we entered an indoor area — the annexe to the gorilla enclosure. The couplet and horizontal inscription at the door caught my eye; some characters are missing, and the surviving strokes read roughly as follows:

> Rainforests together nurture all things as one  
> Great apes share one line, all distant kin  
> Horizontal scroll: the great ape

Once you go in you understand: the couplet is not just a joke, it really is the theme of this pavilion.

![Timber panels headed A Ten-Year Journey recount how Hongshan prepared for its gorillas, with an engraved portrait of Colo.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/85482a2248eac9372508661ad2843e34.webp)

This is one of the rare behind-the-scenes sections in a zoo, telling the story of the first gorilla ever born in a zoo and the gorilla conservation movement it set off in zoos worldwide.

![An orangutan lies resting on a rocky ledge behind a thick timber post and ropes, a grey door with a gridded window beyond.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/b54692b499a43501ff72f8f1bfa6b6c9.webp)

In the indoor house we met an orangutan — often called the red orangutan — the only great ape living in Asia. Its enclosure is not only big but contains water and plenty of climbing frames. This is a male; only males grow the wide cheek pads on both sides of the face.

![In a dim hall a wood-board title asks how to know a bullfrog, a green frog model lit beside it.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/c5127cd3411feaf34d27ae68bc345b78.webp)

On the other side, this apparently incongruous exhibit, How to Know a Bullfrog, is in fact a tribute to an essay titled How to Exhibit a Bullfrog. That essay is regarded as the bible of the zoo world and is the source of the operating philosophy of most modern zoos that care about animal welfare.

![The Nile monitor's display is thickly planted with palms and tall sword-leaf plants, its label at lower left naming Varanus niloticus.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/1b7a202d4d8fc786ca56e476ff6a6983.webp)

The indoor area is not all panels; African animals are interspersed throughout. This is the Nile monitor's display, the monitor hidden in shadow. What amazed me is that they really built a living tropical wetland environment in this tiny space.

![Behind glass, a prep kitchen with a stainless table, stocked shelves and broccoli heads, a handwritten card about gnawed branches at right.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/2df023f227a4df669388dd96c0d8d026.webp)

This looks like a kitchen or workroom where the gorillas' meals are prepared. The process of caring for gorillas is itself worth exhibiting as part of the animals' lives.

![Wooden labels headed gorilla and chimpanzee sit on a mossy dead-branch display hung with drawn plaques, a woman and girl reading them.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/475f3f3a3cd3ea055ade1a448bce870e.webp)

To make up for most visitors not seeing the gorillas, the indoor area invests heavily in gorilla knowledge. From the content itself to the way it is presented, everything works to create an atmosphere that makes you think of gorillas.

![A plywood plaque shows a woodcut-style chimpanzee wading in green water with another on the bank, captioned that they soak to stay cool.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/fe8204be5e683fcdd9259285f97e82b7.webp)

![A woodcut-style orangutan holds a bundle of leaves over its head as an umbrella, the painted note saying apes shelter from rain that way.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/2df236d7e8be33bb82e10f46bfafea83.webp)

![A painted ape hangs from a branch over a river, jabbing a home-made spear into the water to catch fish.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/f9406abda8b11dfa71e5d353ca62c99f.webp)

Cute illustrations introduce the various behaviours of the apes.

![A wooden panel headed Homo explains bipedalism, illustrated by a march of figures from a knuckle-walking ape to an upright human with a stick.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/d35eab8c9232c321a0f136082fb43006.webp)

And of course there is us. Only four kinds of hominid remain: gorillas, chimpanzees, orangutans and humans. Our ancestors' habitat no longer really required climbing trees. Walking upright freed the hands; tool use moved the processing of food outside the body; the energy saved on digestion was, by accident, spent on developing intelligence — and from then on we pulled away, becoming something the other species cannot begin to understand. You could say this evolutionary path is one link in another, but to me it looks more like a chain of coincidences.

![A mural-backed panel traces how people relearned gorillas, through a King Kong poster and a caged museum specimen.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/3fa1767895dcf3be80840689d97bb6b4.webp)

The panels before this tell the history of how humans came to know gorillas. From the earliest image of a monster with a human face and a beast's body, to films such as King Kong, the gorilla's public image has always been negative. Hunters and explorers also killed gorillas to show off their prowess.

![A gorilla skull model with heavy brow and bared canines sits on a table beside a no-touching card and stacked hand-lettered labels.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/408e3a3f60aed61a46ef9dfb2d113641.webp)

A gorilla skull model sits on a small table, and the mood is very much a declassified archive.

![A painted rainforest mural of three women primatologists at work, each paired with a wooden plaque on what her fieldwork revealed.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/ecf0c180ee2550032d1f0b64b60798cd.webp)

The tone shifts to three remarkable women who restored the apes' reputation. The several kinds of ape are in fact gentler than people imagine; a television beside them plays historical footage of these women studying apes deep in their habitat, having built a relationship of deep trust and observing each other at arm's length. Sadly, according to the film, one gorilla that had been followed for a long time was eventually killed by poachers.

![A kerosene lamp and a woven basket holding two potatoes sit on a table, a girl in pink lifting the lamp by its handle.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/64e1738e42418e80335c7a76948a1a7d.webp)

Continuing the archive style, the table holds a kerosene lamp and two potatoes made of foam plastic, reflecting the hard conditions of their research.

![A chimpanzee lies on a straw bed with one arm across its chest, a rope wrapped in green fabric slanting overhead.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/0f502b4112756ba605e236c01078785e.webp)

At the end of the indoor house is a chimpanzee enclosure, where an adult lay resting while a youngster sat beside it playing.

![Wide view of the chimpanzee house: dead trees and ropes lashed into a dense climbing frame, a chimp resting on straw below.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/401cf4eb288c4bace542b3e636a73b35.webp)

Pull the lens back for the full chimpanzee enclosure. Such intricate climbing frames must be rare in Chinese zoos.

Human activity — including activity before the age of civilisation — has drastically changed the natural environment. Large species higher up the food chain suffer most from human impact.

The IUCN Red List divides all animals by extinction risk into the following categories:

**Extinct**  
-   Extinct (EX)
-   Extinct in the wild (EW)

**Threatened**  
-   Critically endangered (CR)
-   Endangered (EN)
-   Vulnerable (VU)

**Lower risk**  
-   Near threatened (NT)
-   Least concern (LC)

Gorillas, chimpanzees and orangutans — our three closest relatives — are all endangered or critically endangered. When you get to know an animal, it is worth checking its conservation status too; you may find that many animals you take for granted sit in the threatened category.

![A dome-shaped shelter shingled with dried banana leaves, a dark doorway gaping, a paper sign noting it copies a Twa forest hut.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/56782e83ae770772e9f4afc1e3876609.webp)

At the exit of the indoor area there is also a temporary shelter of the Twa people, who share the rainforest with the gorillas. The Twa once lived by hunting and gathering and built no permanent homes. Later, because of gorilla conservation, they were forced out of the rainforest and today mostly make a living from tourism. But more and more research shows that the Twa do not threaten the gorillas; they are part of the rainforest ecosystem too.

![A hand-lettered sign on a trunk warns that this is the last toilet before the Africa area, some 20 to 30 minutes on.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/966cfddc911c503152d3031bff820260.webp)

Leaving the primate cluster we came across a thoughtful sign warning that after this point there would be no toilet for a long stretch.

After years as a user-experience designer, with all the methods and principles, in the end it comes down to one thing: think for the user. That is mostly a matter of willingness, not ability. Hongshan Forest Zoo's user experience is better than that of many internet companies.

The path ahead leads to another part of the African area, with non-primates such as giraffes and zebras.

![A chalkboard sets out wildlife tourism's positive and negative effects, with a table of sustainability principles and visitor advice.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/0e5f372d51a7a667832966b95bebd875.webp)

The giraffe enclosure has a blackboard discussing the pros and cons of wildlife tourism for conservation. It is not a black-and-white matter; what counts is how it is done.

![Two giraffes reach up to browse, a reticulated giraffe on the left with crisp patches and a southern giraffe on the right with blurred edges.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/e33b1320c9ceb027c68a27b48c7c9b3c.webp)

At least two kinds of giraffe can be seen here. On the left is a reticulated giraffe, with crisp patches extending down to the lower legs, found in East Africa. On the right is a southern giraffe, its patch edges blurred and slightly jagged, widely distributed in southern Africa.

![An interpretive sign sets four circular coat-patch close-ups side by side, labelled Masai, Southern, Northern and reticulated giraffe](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/83c5714ae13ab847d9982077158189b6.webp)

Research shows that giraffes had already spread into four distinct zones by the Middle Pleistocene, 230,000 to 370,000 years ago, and since then have had almost no gene flow, taking independent evolutionary paths. Coat pattern is a reliable way to tell them apart.

A giraffe's pattern is not only camouflage; it is also an efficient cooling system. Giraffes cannot sweat heavily as humans do, so they had to evolve other mechanisms. Viewed in the thermal infrared band, the dark patches of a giraffe give off far more heat than the pale network between them, because each dark patch is an independent cooling unit: an artery runs straight to its centre, and capillaries then spread out in all directions like the spokes of a bicycle wheel. Blood is cooled efficiently there, then returns to the body and helps cool the whole animal.

![Sprinkler spray drifts over a muddy pond and reed beds in the giraffe enclosure, an animal standing beyond the shade sail](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/0b58c8e395edc94c352c84c5e5c02488.webp)

This enclosure is much bigger than the zebra enclosure at the entrance, and has a waterfall and a small pond.

![A yellow diamond warning sign with a black kangaroo silhouette is fixed to a log post, vines trailing past](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/0e19bc419b0740e9a1748c3f1207da7b.webp)

Near the exit of the South Gate New Area there is also a small Australian area — kangaroos, emus and other Australian specialities. Even the roadside decorations use Australian-style traffic signs, which is a nice little touch.

![Two kangaroos lie on sandy ground in the shade, the nearer one's thick tail stretched out behind it](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/854d249f7b42c11b48245e5a7cd2cb83.webp)

Kangaroos shelter in the shade; their tails are thicker and longer than their hind legs.

Bipedal animals such as kangaroos, theropod dinosaurs and birds are not rare in nature. But unlike human bipedalism, they must grow their bodies so that front and rear are roughly equal in weight, keeping the centre of gravity directly above both legs.

![A painted wooden sign recounts Australia's 2019-2020 Black Summer fires, flames and kangaroo silhouettes crowding its lower edge](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/dbccb72b291c4f4db602238d1f6ea601.webp)

Between 2019 and 2020 Australia had a bushfire season called Black Summer that dealt a heavy blow to its ecology. Reptiles, birds, mammals and frogs all suffered direct casualties and habitat loss to varying degrees. Most Australian kangaroos are still least concern, but some species with limited habitat dropped straight into endangered or critically endangered.

The Australian area has other animals too, but we were hungry and hurried off to eat, passing through quickly and leaving the South Gate New Area.

### Gondwanaland

After lunch we walked the south side of Fangniu Hill, heading straight for Gondwanaland.

![A hand-drawn map on lined paper fits South America, Africa, Antarctica, India and Australia into Gondwana](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/2cab718ed8a7f0750635def233bac2c6.webp)

The name of this area comes from the supercontinent Gondwanaland.

That is one of the legendary stories of geological history. As early as 750 million years ago, the supercontinent Rodinia broke up and dispersed, then reassembled under the action of plate tectonics. Africa and South America formed West Gondwana; the coastlines of the two continents match remarkably well and their species are highly cognate, which helped people establish that continental drift existed. Meanwhile the Indian subcontinent, Antarctica and Oceania formed East Gondwana. The two collided in the Cambrian and formally merged into the ancient continent of Gondwana.

Gondwana then collided with Laurasia to the north, forming the supercontinent Pangaea, when almost all the land on Earth was joined together. After the Jurassic, Pangaea split again; Gondwana separated and continued to break apart. The whole process lasted more than a hundred million years and produced today's continents and oceans.

![Casts of trilobites, dinosaurs and early mammals sit along stacked strata on a timeline wall, watched by a child in a floral hat](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/a9371d3cbbb06bb713a0c51905b858d3.webp)

Inside, the first thing you see is a wall of strata, with representative species of each geological period painted on the appropriate layer. Seeing it, you understand why the area is called Gondwanaland. In fact most creatures from the Gondwana era are no longer to be seen today. What is exhibited here are animals of Central and South America, and South America is precisely the farthest point reached by dominant Eurasian and African species spreading along land bridges. Some species arrived and some did not; South America is a superb window onto the process of evolution. At the same time Gondwanaland is the core area in all of Hongshan for telling the story of how species evolved, and the one that most resembles a museum, unfolding from ancient to modern times.

Our daughter can now recognise a few characters. She saw the two of the Permian and the three of the Triassic and excitedly pointed them out to me. I explained that different geological eras formed different soil layers, older below and newer above, one pressing on the next, just like what Mr Bull sees when he digs up the road in Peppa Pig. We also played a matching game: she found an animal, I told her what it was, and then we followed the strata to the left to see which era it lived in.

![A red-backed bearded saki crouches on a wooden box roof, its heavy tail curled in an arc behind](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/a0289acc48dda73643194f8a821abd4c.webp)

On the other side, beyond the glass, is a broad woodland where red-backed bearded sakis live — an arboreal monkey from South America that almost never comes to the ground. Look at their thick tails, which give them excellent balance when moving through the branches.

![Two red-backed bearded sakis perch on a suspended rope, one clutching leaves, their heavy tails hanging straight down](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/327ef13d3042c7eabf3d1bee3f9ec160.webp)

Their faces are very funny, like a long-bearded monk wearing a wig.

![A black-and-white colobus lies on a timber platform inside a netted open-air enclosure strung with ropes among banana plants](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/8a35a9572511594ade3d26b1d2351c49.webp)

Ahead is an equally vast open-air enclosure. The eastern black-and-white colobus appears again, in a space far larger than at the South Gate New Area, with richer climbing facilities.

![Two eastern black-and-white colobus press together behind the glass, one bowing its head to groom the other](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/3d81b94fb85b05a63a9106001e84a2ef.webp)

They are very much at ease here; two came to the glass and began grooming each other.

![One eastern black-and-white colobus lies on the plank deck with its long white tail plume raised, the other sitting nearby](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/b35f35bfefe3b5aabd22592ce2555a84.webp)

The one being groomed then raised its tail high, signalling the other to keep going.

![Bilingual panel headed Co-Evolution of Plants and Insects marks how plants gained spores, vascular tissue and leaves along a timeline](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/c3c79c12cf38d81e59946cf920f36d3f.webp)

On the wall of the other side the story has already moved on to how plants evolved various structures to overcome environmental limits, climb onto land and grow ever taller.

![Panel on how Lepidodendron grew huge pairs a circular trunk cross-section, vascular bundle called out, with a painted scaly trunk](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/23df1d5adc87a503bada68c643e3d2d2.webp)

The hardcore detail is spelled out too. After plants moved from water onto land, the biggest change was that they used to be soaked in water all over, whereas on land the water is underground and the sunlight is above, so the two have to be gathered separately. To break past the low, creeping form of mosses and gain a competitive edge, they had to fully decouple the collection of water from the collection of sunlight, push the two further apart, grow tall and intercept the sunlight before the low plants get it.

Vascular bundles and lignin were plants' great inventions. The water pipes in the centre of the trunk draw water from the soil into the leaves by transpiration, while the dead cells outside the vascular bundles were injected with lignin, a tough material that glues fibres together — and from then on land plants entered the age of gigantism.

![Panel on Darwin's prediction about the star orchid, with a pinned sphinx moth specimen and drawn white star-shaped blooms below](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/7a442c837582d171207d2b11ff9b5069.webp)

Plants grow tall to survive, but for reproduction they mostly depend on animals. On the island of Madagascar there is a unique orchid, the Darwin's orchid, whose pollen hides deep inside a slender, elongated flower that ordinary insects cannot reach. From his understanding of coevolution, Darwin predicted that some animal with an extremely long proboscis must exist to pollinate it. The prediction was only confirmed after Darwin's death: the coiled proboscis of the African hawk moth, unrolled, can reach the pollen of the Darwin's orchid. Natural evolution is bizarre but follows clear underlying rules.

The next outdoor area displays a large number of animals from the Americas.

![A red-handed tamarin sits upright on a thick branch, its orange-yellow feet conspicuous against black fur](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/df4148c389bfe7a9c292dc0a11dd2d4b.webp)

The red-handed tamarin, a small monkey whose orange-yellow feet are very conspicuous. On their feet only the big toe has a nail; the rest of the digits are sharp claws.

![A golden-headed lion tamarin clings to a leaning branch, its black face ringed by an orange mane, green bamboo behind](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/efa351ed2f652993833302b3bf5ce383.webp)

The golden-headed lion tamarin — the ring of fur around its face really does look like a lion's mane. When in danger they raise their fur to make themselves look bigger. These small monkeys like to live near bromeliads; after rain, small puddles form in the gaps between leaves and trunk, and salamanders, frogs and other animals come up high to breed and forage, providing them with food.

![A red chunk of food is wedged among bamboo stalks and a bare branch, suspended mid-air for the monkeys](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/8fef24b76b3de8a8e53f6a3a1b91d311.webp)

The golden-headed lion tamarins' food is also tied up in mid-air, so they never have to leave the familiar treetops.

![Bilingual panel on Indian tribes' animist philosophy, its text flanked by a river fisherman photo and a mountain valley view](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/3b5982d09ae7119c58de61cd126e3986.webp)

Besides the animals, the exhibit also introduces the indigenous peoples who are part of the local ecology, and their nature beliefs.

![An irregular jigsaw-cut howler monkey board holds adult and infant photos, diet notes and a South America range map](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/73af6d314f3caca1f86d2c4c0dba4a1d.webp)

The animal information boards here mostly use this irregular format, combining photographs and maps, which makes each point easy to take in at a glance.

![Two scarlet ibises perch among green leaves, the upper one standing beside a nest of twigs](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/eb227ce3366cb1f04d3798606f49ede6.webp)

The scarlet ibis, which lives in American wetlands. This bird is black when it hatches — look at the one at the upper right of the photo: in the nest below it a chick is being preened, and it is pitch black. They feed year-round on shrimps and crabs rich in carotenoids, and in eating them they slowly turn themselves red, much like flamingos.

The outdoor part of Gondwanaland ends here, and we entered an indoor area.

![A toco toucan perches on a thick branch, its huge orange bill tipped with black above a white throat](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/147b9b50af9e8509a39602352d4b8d58.webp)

We came face to face with the toco toucan. The big bill is good for eating all kinds of berries and nuts. For a bird, lightness matters greatly; the bill is not as heavy as it looks, and inside it is actually a high-strength network of hollow structures. The bill is even laced with blood vessels, turning it into a large radiator — and when they sleep, they turn their heads 180 degrees and bury the bill in their feathers to reduce heat loss.

![A great curassow stands on leaf litter, glossy black plumage set off by a curly crest and yellow knob at the bill base](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/6447e39a89b7851ff119f65fa4a7ee75.webp)

The great curassow can fly, but prefers to walk on the ground.

These two birds are kept together here, yet in nature their ranges do not overlap. The toco toucan lives mainly east of the Andes in South America, favouring forest edges and savanna. The great curassow is distributed in Central America and northwestern South America, moving through dense forest.

![A green iguana rests on a sloping branch in a misty rock-walled enclosure, a wooden box tucked into the corner](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/8792554b79ebd8c6782a7136ed4154f8.webp)

In another mixed enclosure there is a green iguana, and in the wooden box in the distance a sloth is hiding. In the real natural environment these two animals are neighbours too; letting each see a familiar other makes the setting closer to the wild.

![A sloth hangs by an arm from a branch, its shaggy tan face turned to the camera and dark nose prominent](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/d497d36bba137c7cf3b9f77ee7d7a618.webp)

Another one hides on the high wall. The sloth's evolutionary strategy is extreme low energy consumption, a very long standby time.

Unlike most mammals, which keep a narrow body-temperature range, their temperature can swing widely by several degrees, closer to that of a cold-blooded animal. So they also need to climb to the canopy for long spells to sunbathe. To save energy they do everything slowly, including digesting: food can take a month to be digested, and a third of their body weight is permanently undigested food.

![An orange iguana perches on a wet branch while fine mist drifts down through the humid enclosure](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/f6f9be93b36f6b5eeaf735e92d189b75.webp)

This enclosure is misted with water to raise the humidity. A sloth's fur has many grooves that easily hold moisture, and in such an environment it grows a coat of green algae. A kind of sloth moth lives as a long-term parasite on the sloth; its droppings fertilise the algae, the algae give the sloth camouflage, and the sloth gives the moth a place to live and breed — three species forming a mobile miniature ecosystem.

![A red-tailed boa lies coiled behind a rock ledge, much of its blotched body hidden by leaves and logs](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/fb28ec28151ed45161518a46be134aa6.webp)

In another enclosure a red-tailed boa is coiled on a stone platform in the corner. This snake is ovoviviparous: the eggs hatch inside the mother and live young are born. These large non-venomous constrictors hunt mainly by coiling and squeezing, but contrary to the common belief that they suffocate prey, the main cause of death is that the enormous pressure cuts off blood flow, bringing the circulatory system to a halt and causing shock.

![A squirrel monkey perches on a branch, its white face and yellow-orange legs showing past a big green leaf](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/6aab2d364e9a6574d29c77d86173b1ca.webp)

The enclosure mixes squirrel monkeys with green iguanas. There are no squirrels in Central and South America; squirrel monkeys and marmosets fill the squirrel niche, with similar lifestyles and diets.

![A dark green wall chart links five round display pods with dashed lines, tracing how reptiles came to dominate](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/ad0cedbdcb5027ec568de55118917957.webp)

This indoor area also has plenty of knowledge panels, continuing the evolutionary thread from the outdoor section, and here it has reached the birth of reptiles.

Every species on Earth came from the sea, and they follow one evolutionary trend: shedding direct dependence on the surrounding environment, one thing at a time. Amphibians no longer depend on gills for oxygen and so avoided fierce competition in the water. Reptiles no longer depend on water to lay their eggs, so their range left the vicinity of water and pushed inland. Warm-blooded animals such as birds and mammals no longer depend on sunlight to regulate body temperature, and could move into cold highlands. In this way life blossomed everywhere and took every corner of the land.

Of course, that is only the land. The ocean has its own wonderful story, and that one you have to hear in a dedicated aquarium.

![A backlit mural of the tree of evolution wraps around a corner wall, fine branches crowded with small animal figures](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/71813772f7d84351a3e8fe326a197351.webp)

Few visitors stop at the huge mural in the corner. It is a detailed tree of evolution, showing at a glance which branch each animal is on, who is related to whom, who is older and who is newer — echoing the evolutionary path of shedding dependence I mentioned earlier.

![A shallow dark pool with submerged branches and trailing vines inside the mixed exhibit, reflections on the glass](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/22718c32b9b66b16605f6714571d1e68.webp)

Turning this corner brings you to the high point of Gondwanaland's mixed housing: at least six species live together in this small indoor hall, peacefully.

![A sailfin lizard lies on a rock ledge, its tall dorsal sail raised and long tail trailing down through the leaves](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/9285163a33b1e04f0a15fcb666bef6b9.webp)

A sailfin lizard lies in the high corner. The sail standing up behind it brings Spinosaurus to mind.

![A hill myna perches on a tangle of branches, its black plumage offset by an orange-yellow bill and head wattles](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/980dd29f150c233d3a02c26a86b8cabc.webp)

On a treetop stands a hill myna, famous for its outstanding ability to mimic.

![A Burmese tortoise walks toward the camera over leaf litter, its domed brown shell and scaly head filling the frame](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/cc2f08dd3d0d9d55458055b74ea4b3a2.webp)

A Burmese tortoise made its way to the display window, and I caught it right there.

Another sloth hides in the wooden box high up, too far to see clearly.

![A few small fish drift through green, murky water among submerged branches and floating lily pads](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/5444b48a1bb7c6ff7f628b80ae5d6a09.webp)

Below the water are striped barbs and loaches.

![A Central American agouti sits on reddish soil, a second one blurred mid-stride behind it](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/ab0292c8657c42baf0b40451a85aff5f.webp)

The nearby enclosure holds Central American agoutis. When I wrote about squirrel monkeys I mentioned that Central and South America have no squirrels, and the agouti also performs part of the squirrel's role. They can crack extremely hard nuts and, like squirrels, hoard in seasons of plenty, hiding food everywhere. What they remember they dig up and eat; what they forget becomes accidental planting.

The last stretch of the indoor area holds several kinds of monkey.

![A cotton-top tamarin stares through the branches, its spiky white crest standing out against a black face](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/43eb2ed8af8e2adb28388bf2613448eb.webp)

The cotton-top tamarin, dressed like an indigenous chief. Its range is tiny, limited to the rainforest of northwestern Colombia. It can produce high-frequency calls like a bird, reaching even ultrasonic frequencies that the human ear cannot resolve.

![A common marmoset sits on a thick branch, white ear tufts flaring, a metal feeding basket hanging beside it](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/2a9c3668aa51df01f5aa91c6f872fdd9.webp)

The common marmoset has a wider range and looks like a bald version of the cotton-top tamarin.

![A red-handed tamarin sits side-on along a branch, its orange feet standing out against dark fur and a long hanging tail](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/015eb8efe74a9d7273a705abf5bb6090.webp)

A red-handed tamarin appears here too.

These small monkeys all belong to the marmoset family. Their shared features are a small body and claws on all digits except the big toe, which let them pierce tree trunks and climb vertically — one reason they can fill the squirrel niche. They also almost always give birth to fraternal twins, which is another distinctive trait. They share predators too, and the red-tailed boa we saw earlier is one of them.

![A white-faced saki crouches on a rock ledge high in the enclosure, framed by an overhang and a tree trunk](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/49c50efadfa6b53f8908294f3675132f.webp)

The finale is the star of Hongshan Forest Zoo: the white-faced saki Dudu and his family.

White-faced sakis are monogamous. The male is jet black all over with only the face white, while the female has brown fur with two white strands hanging below the eyes. I am not sure whether the one I photographed is Dudu's wife Huahua or their daughter Peanut.

![Only a motion-blurred dark form remains on the rock ledge, the saki gone in a streak](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/52610c18c6a2409c7ed801feca634545.webp)

Hongshan's animals have the right not to be seen, and almost every enclosure has a corner where they can be completely out of visitors' sight. In the time it took to take this photo, it had already slipped away.

![Five navy display columns, each bearing a scientist's portrait and theory name, rise beside a girl in a floral hat](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/5c9a37fd8d4fb410417f4f0329eef43b.webp)

Before leaving the indoor area, the text panels have essentially finished telling the story of how species evolved, paying tribute here to several scientists who contributed to our understanding of heredity and evolution.

![A tan cut-out of Gondwana's landmasses is studded with circular animal photos beside the title The Biosphere on Earth](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/d7650b4a7a086a1136857cd8302d2126.webp)

To drive the point home, the species of each modern continent are pasted onto their corresponding positions on Gondwanaland.

![Two pink-orange flamingos stand in shallow water, one dipping its hooked bill beneath the surface to feed.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/b5f49552bd4d887b8a2c3e1979d3123e.webp)

Just before the exit of Gondwanaland there is still a sizeable open-air flamingo enclosure. The flamingo is an ancient species, and surprisingly it counts as a close relative of the pelican. Its bill has specialised into a spoon that filters carotenoid-rich algae and small animals out of salt lakes, turning the bird red.

![Two flamingos on bare sand, the paler one on the left tucking its long neck into its back feathers.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/776eaf6d96386e9b7aa55885815dc9f0.webp)

Newly hatched flamingos are grey-white; we saw one whose feathers were not yet that red, so it is probably relatively young.

Before leaving we were lucky enough to witness two flamingos in a dispute. When they fight they crash their bills together like fencers. One collision settles it, and the loser slinks away.

Just the South Gate New Area and Gondwanaland took us until four in the afternoon. With the remaining time we accelerated through two small standalone houses.

### Koala House

![Behind glass, a grey koala wedged in a eucalyptus fork, head down among the leaves.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/9cb243c531690ef429b7d6d2583780e2.webp)

Koalas are my wife's favourite, so this had to be arranged. Because we went late they had already returned indoors to feed, but we could still see them.

![Through glass, the koala house interior: bare eucalyptus trunks fixed to a green floor, tied leaf bunches, two heat lamps glowing.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/2a4053c26e3417243615bfc17cdbe93f.webp)

The indoor enrichment is simple. Considering that for most of its life a koala eats and sleeps in a tree like this, it is probably much like one of us office workers eating a lunch at the desk. But look closely: every branch tip has a black water trough attached, and the eucalyptus leaves, brought from far away, are stood in the trough to stay fresh. Koalas are very fussy about their food.

How did a slow, hefty lump like the koala survive natural selection? An important premise has to be cleared up here: because Australia separated from the other continents early and became effectively isolated, the vast majority of more competitive placental mammals never reached it by natural migration — apart from animals people later brought, such as pet cats, which have dealt some species a devastating blow. So Australia's native mammals — kangaroos, thylacines, koalas — are mainly marsupials. The koala is indeed not very competitive, but there was no sufficiently strong animal to replace it.

The koala also had its species opportunity. Humans arrived in Australia about 45,000 years ago and burned land extensively for farming, changing the landscape and causing a mass extinction of native life. Fire-tolerant eucalyptus gained an advantage in that change and spread everywhere, and the koala, unbothered by the toxicity of eucalyptus leaves, caught that tailwind and instead flourished.

![A black rubber ramp propped against a concrete kerb beside a blue rubberised track, blue-painted characters cut through it.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/bfec912fa6cedc3844c656401f515977.webp)

Leaving the Koala House, we saw this contraption by the road and could not work out what it was for.

![A hand-painted wooden sign for a frog ramp, with a frog drawing and diagrams contrasting a kerb frogs cannot climb and a slope they can.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/17e790b38f3f1a2f8b60a043233e302a.webp)

The sign beside it explains it clearly: it helps young frogs cross the kerb and get back into the woods. Hongshan's care for animals already goes beyond the species on display; the wild animals on the hill are protected too.

### New Tiger Reserve

![A Siberian tiger soaking in a rock-edged pool, only its head and striped back above the water.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/046a244f12b2bb2394e22b02cb5f129c.webp)

With the last bit of time left we chose the New Tiger Reserve, which our daughter might enjoy. When we arrived the Siberian tiger was soaking in the pool. Soaking like that was also the first time I had seen it in a zoo, because most zoos I had been to before did not give their tigers a pool at all.

![A bleached tiger lower jaw laid on the rocks by the pool, its two long canines pointing up.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/88e473007469cd39dd8899e11d49cc67.webp)

A tiger's lower jaw is displayed beside it; set against a live tiger you can imagine its terrifying bite force.

After its bath the tiger walked towards the visitors, brushed past the glass — giving my daughter a fright — and left paw prints in the soft earth.

A whole row of tiger facts hangs on the wall of the walkway. I learned for the first time that tigers have reflective patches behind their ears, so cubs can follow their mother at night.

![An elevated steel corridor on grey columns runs above the treetops, capped by a row of arched wire hoops.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/bb1ce671c00542345cc6542aff89e8df.webp)

![A child in a pink jumper presses both hands into two bronze tiger paw casts on a wooden bench.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/9757931b6a786d695d0b1f49393df41a.webp)

Under one shelter there are a few interactive devices: two tiger paw-print models you can put your hand against for comparison, and a tiger-roar speaker that can be triggered once every five minutes.

![A wooden board lettered in green with a tiger conservation message, a painted tiger head filling the lower right.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/4b7779a1648ad6d254de15c969a7115b.webp)

The conservation slogan even quotes a lyric from Mayday.

Coming out of the New Tiger Reserve, the visit was essentially over. The zoo has no strict clearing time, but the announcement said that to safeguard animal welfare there are no street lights in the zoo, so visitors are advised to leave before dark.

A full day at Hongshan really is of very high quality. If I had gone at my museum pace, the South Gate New Area alone could have taken me a whole day. With our daughter along we were already rushing as much as we could. Through the whole visit, visitors probably also felt and accepted Hongshan's philosophy, forming a virtuous circle — not once did anyone shout or tap on the glass.

And think back to the animals on display here: most are least concern. A good zoo does not have to win on variety or rarity; by showing these species truthfully and three-dimensionally, they have actually reached the state described in How to Exhibit a Bullfrog.

### Xuanwu Lake at Night

After dinner there was still a little time, so we came to Xuanwu Lake. This is Nanjing's own citizens' park, with the Ming city wall nearby. Climbing the wall was out of the question by then, so we just walked along the lake.

![Xuanwu Lake at night, a distant skyline of red, purple and blue lights mirrored in still black water.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/cd90382023109a8a0300f6de5b0057d1.webp)

Xuanwu Lake is not small; it is on the same order of area as Hangzhou's West Lake. The lake is divided by several islands and causeways, so from any angle you only see a small part of it.

Along the shore there are many paths shaded by tall plane trees, some of them over the water. In many places along the lake there is no railing at all, which is rather unusual.

![At night a red ribbon-like sculpture crowns a white stone column above a lit circular basin.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/9f14abbecfc0274fe15a03c4b9989fad.webp)

Wandering around the park we passed this structure: Huazi Garden on Cuizhou Island, a modern monument built for the World Chinese Entrepreneurs Convention. Xuanwu Lake itself is very old, and Republican-era buildings are scattered through the park, encountered by chance while strolling — a fitting appetiser for the next day's Republican theme.

## Day 2: Zhongshan

Since my wife had specified the Republican theme, Zhongshan was a must. Here are Meiling Palace and the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum, one of the twin cores of Nanjing's Republican history; the other core is the Presidential Palace and the Yihe Road mansion district, scheduled for the following day.

### Meiling Palace

![Meiling Palace's ochre walls and green glazed eaves show through tall plane trees as visitors climb the steps.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/91015ffaf21acb0bb34d75e201f129f5.webp)

Meiling Palace is often popularly believed to be a private villa that Chiang Kai-shek gave to Soong Mei-ling, but in fact it was conceived from the planning stage as the residence of the chairman of the Nationalist government. Completed in 1934, later than the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum, it was refused by the then chairman Lin Sen — a figurehead head of state — who would not move into such a luxurious residence. The building therefore served as a place for dignitaries to rest after paying their respects at the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum, and its official name has always been the former residence of the chairman of the Nationalist government.

It was only when Chiang Kai-shek, chairman of the Military Affairs Commission and holder of real power, took it over for his own use together with his wife Soong Mei-ling that the building's actual function changed. After the victory in the War of Resistance, during the civil war, Soong Mei-ling hosted a great deal of foreign affairs here and the interior was stamped with her personal mark, so the popular name Meiling Palace was finally legitimised and became the official name used for visitors.

![Green glazed eaves and painted beams of the car porch above red columns, small ridge figures against the trees.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/9764e611a47bc110ace742216d55221c.webp)

Meiling Palace is a building fusing Eastern and Western styles. This is its carriage porch, built in a traditional Chinese architectural form.

![A black vintage sedan parked under the car porch, its blue military plate reading 00385, framed by painted beams.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/a52731e77417dd36ff397a9e04b365a8.webp)

In the 1930s, the motorcar age arrived for senior official residences and public buildings alike, and this kind of carriage porch grew up naturally so that one could step out of the car without getting wet. The drop-off zones of modern luxury hotels, stations and airports all developed from it.

![Looking up at the ochre brick wall and tall steel windows, a painted bracket band under the eaves and a white marble balustrade.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/79a3e3c104bb9c7d9145b3bff46b4ff6.webp)

The whole building is roofed with green glazed tiles in a double-eaved hip-and-gable style. After Puyi abdicated in 1912, the architectural forms of the Qing empire no longer carried any legitimacy as regulation, but as symbols of Chinese culture they became the heritage of later generations in a purer form.

![The reception room's dark wood doors and green curtains, with a bilingual Reception Room label in the foreground.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/70c76d15afae3b12c480c8a343eea5ab.webp)

The interior of Meiling Palace restores how it looked when the Chiangs lived here. This is the reception room at the side of the ground-floor hall, where visitors waited to be received.

![Visitors in motion climb a wide stone staircase, a framed landscape scroll hanging on the wall beside it.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/31bf115c4890e93dd90855ce66f0760f.webp)

In the centre of the ground-floor hall, the staircase faces you as you come in the main door, and the flight rises, then splits left and right to the upper floors. Split, merge, split, merge, repeating, strictly symmetrical — it has a powerful sense of ceremony.

Architecturally this form is called an imperial staircase, and those born in the 1970s and 1980s should find it very familiar. In the 1980s and 1990s, before lifts were widespread, government office buildings, municipal venues and department stores mostly looked like this, springing up everywhere, and many third- and fourth-tier cities still have plenty today. This insistence on left-right symmetry was also influenced by neoclassical architecture, then imported into China through standardisation in the former Soviet Union. This is the Western side of Meiling Palace's architecture.

![A cast-iron radiator behind a dark wooden grille at the wall base, with a patterned tile border on the floor.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/aaf0493924a7a937fe4a6c843e99260a.webp)

Up the stairs, there are radiators at the base of the wall. This is a heating vent connected to a heating system that serves the whole building; such vents are found throughout the second and third floors of Meiling Palace.

![A long banquet table laid with lace, gold-rimmed plates and flowers, landscape paintings above the mantel.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/69af44b56fae54c6183efc7933253247.webp)

The centre of the second floor is the banqueting hall, used for official dinners and receptions for foreign affairs.

Note that the wall is in the typical European neoclassical three-part structure. At the bottom is a waist-high wooden wainscot to protect against dirt. The main wall is khaki paint, which does not run all the way to the ceiling but is separated by a dark red wooden line, with the blank band above it in the same colour as the ceiling. That wooden line is a picture rail, grooved so metal hooks can hang from it. You can just make out in the photo that every painting on the wall hangs from that rail, so no holes have to be drilled and no nails hammered anywhere in the house. Expansion bolts did not exist yet; one blow of a nail and the plaster would soon crumble and fall.

This is also a product of the same era as the imperial staircase, and friends who lived in public housing in the 1980s and 1990s will have been reminded of something. Exactly: this technique also reached Chinese homes by way of the former Soviet Union. When I was small, my grandmother's family lived in the Hongdu Machinery Factory dormitories in Nanchang, a military aircraft plant built with Soviet expert assistance that later also made civilian goods; my grandfather even took part in building the Q-5 ground-attack aircraft. The dormitories in that plant compound were uniformly Soviet blue-brick tile-roofed houses, quite out of keeping with the local red-brick houses outside the compound. The wall arrangement inside those dormitories looked exactly like this, with picture rails too, except that the main wall was also white paint — that era's version of a decorated flat.

![A brass-edged glass panel set flush with the parquet, revealing the cast-iron grille of a floor heating vent.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/207d6b57d546756083caca90e6675306.webp)

The floor of the banqueting hall also has heating vents.

![Second-floor chequerboard tiles in brown and white, split by diagonal bands of window shadow.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/d02a6747a53491e63bbfdbce934718a2.webp)

The patterned floor tiles of the second floor in the light. Of course I never lived through the Republican era, but this image stirred many memories of the 1990s, at least fifty years removed from Meiling Palace. From this angle you can understand how far ahead of its time Meiling Palace was.

![The drawing room's floral carpet and lace-covered armchairs facing a black grand piano by the window.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/94a9511a1a6bc4dd9728f320038b814c.webp)

Beside the banqueting hall is the drawing room. The piano inside is said to have been for the exclusive use of the three Soong sisters.

![Paired round columns framing an arch, exhibition panels of elevation drawings, contracts and an aerial photo between them.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/ab7cf848339b5ae95c3541992714816c.webp)

The space on one side of the second floor has now been turned into a history gallery. The paired Roman columns are a detail in miniature: the interior of Meiling Palace is Western in style overall.

![A display-panel photograph captioned as the villa soon after completion, the building standing behind leafless trees.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/d6bb2d5c07733963731e96c1e317da51.webp)

Meiling Palace was built during the Republican era's Golden Decade. After the two Northern Expeditions and the Northeast Flag Replacement, the various regional warlord factions nominally unified under the Nanjing Nationalist government and stopped fighting one another, and the Republic of China entered a period of economic development.

At that time the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum had been completed only recently, and a number of ancillary buildings had been put up around the whole mausoleum area. In 1930 Chiang Kai-shek proposed setting aside this plot for a rest house, and construction began with the agreement of the mausoleum's management committee. The original purpose of the building was simply a stopover for paying respects at the tomb. But during construction the project kept being scaled up, from a brief rest stop to comfortable living, gradually becoming a residence for the highest executive on the model of the White House and the Élysée.

Note that the growth of this Golden Decade was not evenly spread across the country. What actually developed was the middle and lower Yangtze region controlled by Chiang Kai-shek's Whampoa army. Although by the international definition it was still the eve of the Second World War, in fact China's Second World War had long since begun. The Northeast had already fallen, Japan had not yet invaded in full, and the storm was gathering.

![Meiling Palace's double-eaved roof seen head-on, tiers of green glazed tiles above rows of painted brackets.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/fda07aadf2af8eb04b553a180cd30d5a.webp)

A door in the banqueting hall leads to the second-floor terrace, from which the Chinese roof of Meiling Palace can be seen more clearly.

![Green glazed eaves and red rafters turning the corner of the ochre wall, a dense plane tree canopy behind.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/ebed809cda4c09a1611d7aaefc94395d.webp)

Another feature of Meiling Palace is the plane-tree necklace. If you have seen aerial photographs of Meiling Palace you will notice that the building is ringed by a circle of plane trees, and that ring connects to a long avenue of plane trees to form the shape of a necklace. Modern people like to fit this shape to Soong Mei-ling's aura as a national goddess, but in fact it is pure coincidence.

Of course, plane trees and Meiling Palace really do suit each other. Their leaves are large, and the slightest breeze sets them moving. Behind this historic building they serve as a background of gentle motion, quiet and soft.

![A white marble baluster post carved with a phoenix in the foreground, the terrace railing receding behind it.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/0bf2cec9ca15419a557c9930f2afbd96.webp)

The white marble baluster posts of the terrace, carved with phoenix motifs — the terrace is accordingly named the Phoenix Viewing Terrace.

But since Meiling Palace was not originally built for Soong Mei-ling, the meaning of these phoenix carvings should not be pinned on her either. The designers and builders of Meiling Palace were Chiang Kai-shek's trusted men, and we may guess at certain unspoken thoughts, but not treat them as historical fact.

![Sun Yat-sen's portrait centred on the wall between vertical couplets, a dark desk with a telephone in front.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/a50f2b986f5c2559e748a07d2d71cb28.webp)

Behind the drawing room is Chiang Kai-shek's office. A portrait of Sun Yat-sen was standard equipment in Nationalist government offices at the time, but for anyone who knows modern history, what stands before you is precisely the most dramatic scene of all.

Let me briefly recount the course of events from the 1911 Revolution onward:

> The Wuchang Uprising broke out. It set the precedent of liberating the thinking of workers and peasants while avoiding an immediate assault on the interests of local strongmen and the colonial powers, and it won support or tacit consent from society at every level, at home and abroad. The model was quickly copied by the southern provinces, and the provinces that successfully declared independence jointly honoured Sun Yat-sen as the leader of the revolution.
> 
> The Qing government called out Yuan Shikai to march south and suppress it, but Yuan's troops obeyed only him. He had his own calculations: after sweeping all before him and taking Hanyang, he halted. He had shown his strength and could now in turn hold Empress Dowager Longyu in check. North and south deadlocked, and the south proposed that if Yuan could force the Qing emperor to abdicate and end the feudal dynasty, Sun Yat-sen would yield the provisional presidency to him.
> 
> Yuan Shikai agreed. By then the Qing government was as thin as a feather, and a breath would bring it down. The south did not trust Yuan and tried to constrain him with the institutional power of a parliament, but after the assassination of Song Jiaoren that plan collapsed. Seeing the fruits of the revolution about to be taken entirely by Yuan Shikai, north and south split again, and China entered more than a decade of warlord chaos.
> 
> Those ten-odd years of confusion occurred mainly inside each side. In the north, after Yuan Shikai declared himself emperor he alienated everyone and soon died in despair. The northern warlords, deprived of a common leader, split into the Zhili, Anhui and Fengtian cliques, each backed by a different foreign power, and fought repeatedly. In the south, local strongmen and warlord factions waved the revolutionary banner on the surface while in fact becoming too powerful to control, and the Nationalist government was betrayed again and again.
> 
> Sun Yat-sen realised that unity could never be achieved by co-opting and buying off warlords to fight other warlords. In 1924 he founded the Whampoa Military Academy with Chiang Kai-shek as commandant. Through it Sun wanted to forge an army of the party that truly carried the revolutionary mission.
> 
> Sun Yat-sen did not live to see the result: he died of illness from overwork before the Whampoa army's first Northern Expedition. In the few years after his death Chiang Kai-shek led the Whampoa army through two Northern Expeditions and finally absorbed the regional warlords on the surface, unifying the Republic of China under a single flag.
> 
> But during this period Chiang Kai-shek also drifted further and further from Sun Yat-sen's ideals. After taking Shanghai and Jiangsu-Zhejiang he won the backing of local financial cliques and of British and American interests, freeing himself financially from dependence on the Soviet Union. In exchange he turned on the workers' and peasants' movement and the Communists to protect the interests of local financial cliques and the foreign concessions, an event known as the April 12 Incident. After that the Wuhan Nationalist government broke with Chiang, who set up a separate government in Nanjing.
> 
> Not long afterwards Stalin, through the Comintern, instructed the Chinese Communist Party to launch a full land movement and build workers' and peasants' armed forces. Wang Jingwei of the Wuhan Nationalist government saw this information, grew wary, and also began purging and killing Communists. The first cooperation between the Kuomintang and the Communists broke down completely.
> 
> In this way Wuhan and Nanjing actually came to agree in their attitude towards the Communists. The two merged again, an event known as the Ninghan merger, and the Wuhan government moved to Nanjing. By then the Nanjing Nationalist government had in substance become a warlord force, entirely departing from Sun Yat-sen's revolutionary ideals in its internal governance.
> 
> After that came the Xi'an Incident and the second cooperation between the Kuomintang and the Communists, the Marco Polo Bridge Incident and Japan's full invasion, the end of the Second World War and Japan's surrender, and the civil war — all of which everyone knows well.

Back to this office of Chiang Kai-shek in Meiling Palace: it shows the furnishings of the period after Japan's surrender when he returned to work in Nanjing. The portrait of Sun Yat-sen behind him was merely a political banner.

![The confidential secretary's office with a green-shaded lamp on the desk, a typewriter to one side, its label in front.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/28c2422d8f29f09c6194092d1a3cae5e.webp)

At the head of the stairs to the third floor there is a small confidential secretaries' room, also next door to Chiang Kai-shek's office for prompt contact.

![A framed, yellowed old map of China on the wall, its territory and marginal legend printed in sepia.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/af431f466eb67fc4f282a0ef67d97c69.webp)

Chiang Kai-shek's study on the third floor, with this map of the Republic of China hanging in it.

If the second floor is the reception and office area, the third floor is the Chiangs' private living space. One room has been turned into a display of Republican-era objects: typewriters, microphones, film projectors, telegraph sets and so on.

![Top view of an antique index typewriter, its round typewheel ringed by metal rods above keys marked C1 and SPACES.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/a802f4b8fd17b171fa7ba12f2ce0058b.webp)

![Two vintage microphones side by side, one ring-suspended in a metal hoop, the other hung by white cords inside a grey ring.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/8b7fefb57f81b102e45ed96e70cb2af2.webp)

In the centre of the third floor is a small chapel. The Soong family were Christian, and one of the conditions on which Chiang Kai-shek married Soong Mei-ling was that he embrace Christianity. After moving into Meiling Palace he often came here to pray.

![A dense crowd of visitors peers through a dark wooden door frame at a framed portrait on the wall beyond.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/74df1ae869804a3e7147e13ab9341abc.webp)

Beside it are two bedrooms, packed with people, which we did not enter.

Chiang Kai-shek and Soong Mei-ling slept in separate rooms. Soong Mei-ling often stayed up late watching films, painting or handling foreign affairs, while Chiang kept the routine of a soldier; according to his attendants he suffered severe neurasthenia, woke easily at night and was demanding about his sleeping environment. Soong Mei-ling, meanwhile, had chronic skin allergies and often had to wake at night to apply ointment. The two did not disturb each other.

This is their private dining room. Even at table the two could not agree: Soong Mei-ling, used to Western culture, ate Western food, while Chiang Kai-shek preferred Ningbo dishes such as braised pork with preserved mustard greens.

Leaving the upper floors, we went down to the basement to see how the support staff lived here.

Checking the sources, it turns out that before the Nationalist troops withdrew the exterior of Meiling Palace was painted black all over, which let it escape Japanese bombing, though it still suffered severe deliberate damage during the occupation.

According to the historical record, Chiang Kai-shek rarely stayed here before the War of Resistance. Only after Japan's surrender, when he returned to Nanjing from Chongqing, did he begin to live at Meiling Palace for long periods.

### Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum

![The Bo'ai stone paifang with blue glazed tile roofs and cloud-pattern carving, framed by tall cedars.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/528d4fb84db743448d158cc0d98c8c9e.webp)

The next stop was the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum: a long slope running from the foot of the hill up to the memorial hall halfway up. This photo is very deceptive; in reality there were a great many visitors.

![Crowds pass through the carved stone Bo'ai archway, its opening framing the blue-tiled mausoleum gate.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/a6fabe0e8bcce9079fef83ac58e736ca.webp)

At the outer end of the slope stands this Paifang archway inscribed with Universal Love, the main gate of the whole mausoleum.

In traditional Chinese architecture, glazed tiles of different colours carry different meanings. Yellow was for the emperor himself or for sages of Confucius's stature, green for imperial kinsmen, and blue for sacrifices to heaven. But the blue used here at the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum is not the same matter as the Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests at the Temple of Heaven; it has nothing to do with feudal ritual. It can be read as an echo of the Blue Sky White Sun flag, and as a match for the surrounding natural setting.

![The blue-roofed mausoleum gate with three arched openings, cedars framing the crowded slope beneath it.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/6d8eb86c45c25d3d589674bddcc2229b.webp)

Because of the intermediate mausoleum gate, the steps and the entrance to the memorial hall cannot be seen from the bottom of the slope.

![Wide stone steps packed with visitors climbing toward the blue-roofed memorial hall under a blue sky.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/17bc3b1b559382c4c0518549ea839135.webp)

Through the gate are the famous 392 steps. Making use of the lie of the hill, the atmosphere is solemn.

![A close view of the bronze ding, its cast inscription beside a punctured dent, verdigris mottling the metal.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/4d60dfe699cc6e61cf8d0b2f62385db1.webp)

I had the same question about the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum as about Meiling Palace: how did it escape destruction during the Japanese occupation? The bronze ding on the slope is the answer. When the Japanese attacked Nanjing, the defending troops used the terrain of the mausoleum to mount a defence, and the two sides fought a fierce battle directly inside the grounds.

Throughout the occupation Japan kept something in reserve. As the War of Resistance entered its stalemated phase, the Japanese advance slowed and the Nationalist army faced Japan in several years of standoff on the frontal battlefield. By then Japan's main thrust had turned towards the Communist base areas behind the lines, and it always kept a channel open for persuading the Nationalist army to surrender. The Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum was a spiritual totem, and destroying it would have closed off that possibility entirely.

![The memorial hall facade, a vertical plaque over carved archivolts and gold inscriptions above three arched doors.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/73663c45df8df80a00e7dd7c7fb03abe.webp)

Climbing the steps, you reach the memorial hall. Above is the stone plaque reading Righteousness of Heaven and Earth, and the Three Principles of the People — nationalism, livelihood and democracy — are carved into the stone walls.

The mausoleum architecture also fuses Chinese and Western styles: the ornament above the doorway is clearly Baroque, while above that there is the form of a Chinese paifang.

![Carved stone dougong brackets beneath the eaves, with floral relief filling the triangular gable above.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/a85f2792241abcbd4c271b14ccf844f3.webp)

On the side of the memorial hall, stone reproduces the look of dougong brackets, along with traditional-style gables.

![Gnarled old pine trunks with deeply furrowed bark lean in front of a pale stone wall.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/84551d0473f7771ab27d7270f8973b7c.webp)

Photography is forbidden inside the memorial hall, and to get in you go round a corridor at the side. Along it are ancient lacebark pines, highly consonant with the atmosphere of the cemetery.

![Looking down from the memorial hall terrace over crowded paving toward a hazy city skyline beyond the forest.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/a97c84f968365742b86301f3e35790d3.webp)

In front of the memorial hall, looking down over a large stretch of eastern Nanjing.

![A little girl in a floral bucket hat sits on the smooth stone steps, her face covered by a smiley sticker.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/601b97e7ddc04474f15426e0d2949fb9.webp)

This place holds little attraction for small children; the only thing my daughter liked was the slope beside the steps, worn extremely smooth.

But think the other way round: children can understand none of this and still play slide on the slope here. That contrast also produces a feeling of profound respect. Mr Sun Yat-sen had not a single soldier of his own; relying on the appeal of a lofty ideal, he mobilised forces on all sides to unify China, and it underpins all the good things of later times — including the joy of this slide.

### Music Stage

![The semicircular Music Stage packed with people on wooden benches, the stage wall and pond to the left.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/0da37eb7e46b582d9f57398eb4851051.webp)

Coming down from the mausoleum, the Music Stage is not far away. Fountains, music and the shapes of doves fill the place with the romantic atmosphere of the Republican era.

![A pigeon banks mid-flight against a mackerel sky, the dark stone balustrade cutting the frame.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/81c9b9022ba9d3e76244339e29757a12.webp)

What modern people see through the fog of history is romance, and that is the selective bias of film and television. When you think of the Republican era, what images come to mind? If you are a woman, either the daughter of an overseas-educated financial magnate or a socialite of high society, and at the very least a female student of the Revolutionary Alliance. If a man, either a warlord general or a business prodigy, and at the very least a pioneer of the workers' and peasants' movement. Intellectual emancipation, gilded decadence, storm and stress, the times making the hero — modern people always project one of these identities onto themselves.

In fact, according to historical data, land concentration in the Republican era was comparable to that of the Ming and Qing and had barely improved. Because of the wars, ordinary people's lives were harder still, and the wealth gap was even wider. There was also man-made disaster: Sichuan warlords, for instance, collected land tax from peasants decades in advance.

If you were reborn into the Republican era, the probability of landing in one of those identities would be vanishingly small. Becoming a refugee, by contrast, would be quite likely. The Wanderings of Sanmao is much closer to how ordinary people actually lived in the Republican era; there was nothing romantic about it. I have no wish at all to go back to that time.

### The 1912 Block

After dark we left Zhongshan and went to the 1912 Block near the hotel to find food. It was only a few metro stops, but we were squeezed in for almost half an hour before we could get on.

A genius from the capital of Zhejiang province went to the capital of Jiangsu supposing it was an ordinary off-peak weekend outing. How could it not occur to him that Zhongshan is to Nanjing what West Lake is to Hangzhou? The result was a taste of the Nanjing version of Hangzhou's Longxiangqiao. Ah, who could this genius be — so hard to guess.

![A creeper-covered brick clock tower lit warm at night, an ornamental 1912 sign above the dial.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/9b70cea7b8e4cd5dbc6bdb8b22fbdeeb.webp)

The 1912 Block has a small amount of genuine Republican-era architecture set off by a great deal of modern faux-Republican building, and it looks seamless.

![A lamplit row of grey-brick facades in the 1912 Block, a figure in a black outfit and white hat in front.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/721d8d82954f3c33dac29c12cac413b4.webp)

That day was Halloween, and Nanjing has some cosplay atmosphere too, though not especially strong. After dinner we strolled about and saw a few cosplayers in the block: a little winged demon, a Victorian young lady, and a female agent in a long gown and black bowler hat.

![An exhibition board on Nanjing's 1907 city railway, laid out with text panels, old photos and a route map.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/5cff86d11d4ced159397d8ab40fd0414.webp)

A railway track runs through the 1912 Block, and there is a historical story behind it. In 1907 the Qing viceroy of Liangjiang built a railway through the city of Nanjing; this urban line was a highly advanced piece of engineering at the time, equivalent to a light-rail system. In 1912, when Sun Yat-sen came to Nanjing to take office as provisional president, he travelled on the little train. Later, because of certain drawbacks, the track system was abandoned. Only in 2008, when two lengths of old rail were dug up at a Nanjing construction site, was the course of that old line confirmed.

### The Presidential Palace, Central Route

![A two-storey grey-brick government building with white columns, stepped bracket ends and a patterned railing.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/6d1814fd9d1aee0b75d485fefcf00e58.webp)

The entrance to the scenic area is at the Nationalist Government's Directorate-General of Budget, Accounting and Statistics, a small courtyard enclosed by Republican-era buildings on all four sides, where the first history gallery also begins.

We had very little time today, so we concentrated on the architecture and skipped the text-heavy material.

![A bronze-coloured scale model of the Presidential Palace, dense tiled roofs inside walled courtyards.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/c6e87ce9e430c971465a7ec4be7f2e6e.webp)

In the courtyard of the Directorate-General there is a scale model of the Presidential Palace scenic area. The interior is laid out in three north-south main routes — east, central and west — and the visit essentially works by looking at buildings and gardens from the main routes and at history galleries inside the buildings.

![The Presidential Palace main gate wrapped in blue construction netting and scaffolding behind a green hoarding.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/5b1d6bc3b6d360a5341819820fd57dc8.webp)

Coming in at the Directorate-General, the Presidential Palace's main gate is on your right; it is currently under maintenance. I have seen photographs online, and the gate should be a real highlight: Western classical architecture.

![The gatehouse's gold characters above Ionic columns with fluted shafts and volute capitals around an arch.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/6b6b3b093ceec16912efeeaa17ef4b35.webp)

Borrowing a photo from the internet. The outer side of the Presidential Palace gatehouse has eight Ionic columns, with voluted capitals and gaps between the flutes of the shafts.

Of the three classical Greek orders, the minimalist Doric order presents a vigorous, robust style, typified by the Parthenon in Athens. The Ionic column is more elegant and graceful, typified by the Erechtheion in Athens.

![Red columns rise to a dark rafted ceiling in the Great Hall, oil paintings hung along both side walls.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/aff63101b1cf89518fcea03b116d278b.webp)

This is the main passage of the central route, facing the Great Hall. In the middle is the plaque reading The World Is for All, and on the walls to either side hang six oil paintings — 1, 2, 3 from left to right and 4, 5, 6 from right to left — corresponding in order to six historical events that took place at the Presidential Palace:

1. In 1853 the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom captured Jiangning (Nanjing), renamed it Tianjing, made it the capital and began building the Heavenly Court Palace on a grand scale.
2. In the Qing dynasty this was the yamen of the Viceroy of Liangjiang, governing Jiangsu, Anhui and Jiangxi; successive viceroys are painted together across time.
3. In 1912 Sun Yat-sen took office in Nanjing as provisional president of the Republic of China.
4. In 1937, when the War of Resistance broke out, the Nationalist government announced here its relocation west to Chongqing.
5. In 1946, during the Kuomintang-Communist peace talks in Nanjing, Zhou Enlai fought here for domestic peace.
6. In 1949 Nanjing was liberated in the Yangtze River Crossing Campaign, and sunlight fell through the windows onto the corridor of the Presidential Palace.

One Presidential Palace spans the whole of modern history from the late Qing to Liberation.

![A small bricked-up reception window under a perspex cover, its black bilingual label mounted beside it.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/1d77e64e2b239a888bf3d09b32bd5641.webp)

The buildings of the Presidential Palace are a mixture of legacies from different eras. Beside one of the doors coming in from the Great Hall there still remains a message window from the Qing era of the Viceroy of Liangjiang; at night, when the hall doors were shut, messages were passed through this small window.

![A restored drawing room with black leather sofas, tea sets, Sun Yat-sen's portrait and Republican flags.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/f0072f4e34e381481343a6aff0c87153.webp)

To either side are drawing rooms, restored to how they looked in the Nanjing Nationalist government period.

![Wax figures in a changshan and cheongsams sit talking in the drawing room beneath a framed portrait.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/f4d1d86702a1662c8a91f8697aa593a4.webp)

There are even lifelike wax figures.

![The assembly hall's stage with burgundy velvet curtains and a Sun Yat-sen portrait above a mosaic floor.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/1e9291a9c226707dd9532431119a0157.webp)

The assembly hall, where daily meetings and major ceremonies were held.

![Looking through the red Qilin Gate into a long corridor of red pillars hung with lanterns.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/f77d7fe42874eb1014e12e2d841b7414.webp)

Further in, at the Second Hall, there is another red door, called the Qilin Gate. The old gate no longer exists; this is a modern reconstruction, but the foundations below are still preserved. Inside is Republican-era architecture, connected to a Qing corridor.

![The Zichao Building's plain buff-brick facade and stepped central tower, visitors climbing the entry steps.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/9ada4bcd9c5b1ec1f8afdb661c4c23c5.webp)

Passing through the buildings to the open air brings you to the Zichao Building at the far end of the central route, named after Lin Sen, chairman of the Nationalist government, whose courtesy name was Zichao. The design is fairly functional, with very little carved eave decoration.

Soon after the Zichao Building was completed, the full-scale War of Resistance broke out and the Nationalist government moved west to Chongqing. After the war ended Chiang Kai-shek returned to work here, and it became the administrative hub of the Presidential Palace.

![Dark panelling around the presidential office, a green-topped desk with telephone and Chiang's portrait in uniform.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/94abbdcb02d82c94b4186e03f19942cc.webp)

A portrait of Chiang Kai-shek hangs in the presidential office on the second floor. In 1948, at the National Assembly under the new constitution, Chiang became the first president of the Republic of China. Before that, the office of president did not legally exist in the Nationalist government; the head was called chairman.

After the two Northern Expeditions Chiang Kai-shek became chairman of the Nationalist government. Then, as factional struggles among the party's warlords intensified, Chiang stepped down and Lin Sen took over for twelve years. After Lin Sen died, Chiang took the chairmanship again. At the end of 1947 the Constitution of the Republic of China came into force, the Nationalist government declared the end of the period of tutelage and the start of constitutional rule, and the head of state changed from chairman to president.

![The vice president's office, a green-topped desk stacked with books and mustard armchairs by a tall window.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/c12d36e6250f037b2bd9dd078e9d7ad2.webp)

The office of the vice president, Li Zongren.

Li Zongren was a very complex figure. Leader of the Guangxi clique, he and Chiang Kai-shek joined and split throughout the Republican era. He it was who allied with Feng Yuxiang and Yan Xishan to form the anti-Chiang coalition and launch the Central Plains War; he it was who joined the Nationalist government to fight Japan together and won the Taierzhuang victory; he it was who, after Chiang stepped down, was pushed into the presidency as a puppet; and he it was who, after losing influence in the party in Taiwan, finally returned to the mainland. Throughout this whole historical period he was the strongest rival force to Chiang Kai-shek within the party.

![The old Otis lift, its black double doors beneath a semicircular floor-indicator dial.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/429e7f9025e3df1c75da0fe0b0973e6c.webp)

The Zichao Building also preserves an early Otis lift in good condition.

### The Presidential Palace, Eastern Route

Coming out of the Zichao Building we reached the eastern route, walking from the inside out.

![A plain opening in a cracked whitewashed wall reveals the shelter's dark concrete stairway.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/faca5a9ec29e840223d1fe8136e1a15f.webp)

Passing a small garden, there is an inconspicuous air-raid shelter of the Presidential Palace by the path. After the Battle of Shanghai broke out the Japanese bombed Nanjing frequently; this shelter had its own water, electricity and ventilation, and stood only a dozen metres from the chairman's office.

![Gilt characters reading Executive Yuan on the grey concrete front of the North Building, mirrored in the long pool before it.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/05395b8a32e6236056f5e75a521f9098.webp)

Further east brings you to this low building, the North Building of the Executive Yuan. It now houses the offices of the staff who run the Presidential Palace as a visitor site.

![Taihu rocks piled before a whitewashed garden wall with a round arch and lattice window, grey tiles above.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/516745587f854b084228216cf163c536.webp)

In the middle section of the eastern route there is a pocket garden called Fu Garden. The garden can be traced back to the Heavenly Court Palace of the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom, whose East Garden it was. It was destroyed in the fighting of the late Qing by the Xiang army; after the war Zeng Guofan presided over the restoration of the West Garden, while the East Garden was not rebuilt and became ordinary attached land. In the Republican era some office buildings were put up here. Only in 2003, when the Presidential Palace was comprehensively restored, did the heritage authorities reconstruct the Qing appearance as far as the sources allowed, and only then did the name Fu Garden exist.

![A small tree grows from a moss-covered rock in the middle of a still green pond.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/3b9da80bd75a3c539b209d1e79e68dbf.webp)

The garden does contain some very delicate details.

![Beyond a Qing-style grey tiled roof, a red-tiled Western building with a dormer window rises over the garden wall.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/87d964602636bc8e2892cf46d86850b6.webp)

Standing inside a Qing-style garden with Republican-era Western buildings beyond the wall is a strange feeling, and it runs through the whole Presidential Palace site.

Further south is the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom themed area.

![Scale model of the Heavenly Court Palace, yellow-roofed halls ranked along a central axis behind the round Tianfu Terrace.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/bb430c2e92c6dae1f4da99dec42e7cf8.webp)

After the Taiping army took Jiangning (Nanjing) they made their capital here and renamed it Tianjing. You can see that Hong Xiuquan really treated it as a permanent capital. The planned Heavenly Court Palace, judging only by the area enclosed by its outer city, the Sun City, was even larger than the Forbidden City. Of course, a hastily built imperial city fell far short of the Forbidden City in both construction quality and density.

![Replica of Hong Xiuquan's gilded dragon throne beneath a red plaque reading Taiping Unity, flanked by two crane figures.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/a7022f49ff3dfecb09db25a47a4dc899.webp)

A replica of Hong Xiuquan's dragon throne. Although the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom's banner came from Christianity, limited by the times and their outlook, the regime's political and cultural imagination never really escaped the framework of China's traditional feudal monarchy.

Looking at the inscriptions on the plaques either side, it really does not look like a serious regime, and seems somewhat lacking in culture. Hong Xiuquan wrote poetry himself, and by later assessments it was pure doggerel.

![A pale yellow dragon robe worked in silver dragons and clouds, displayed in a glass case above its matching cap.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/9e48c2097ed20c2b019410f2b5dd5ac9.webp)

The style of Hong Xiuquan's dragon robe.

![A square vermilion impression of Hong Xiuquan's jade seal on aged paper, its columns of characters ringed by dragons and waves.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/9011f4a469f6592b808f2dbcd2a1d814.webp)

Hong Xiuquan's jade seal, strongly theocratic in character.

![A circular dragon relief set into a plain white wall, framed by red columns and a red lattice railing.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/35087ed498fea24ad20750e9f8a61a56.webp)

After the Heavenly King's Palace was completed, Hong Xiuquan never left it again, gradually drifting away from the whole Taiping cause on an island of personal cult.

There is more to the eastern route, but we did not look closely; we cut straight through the central route to the western one.

### The Presidential Palace, Western Route

![A jagged Taihu rockery at the Xu Garden entrance, with a stone table on the cobbled path in front.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/e525220410213076f0d7d61bbba73179.webp)

Coming out, you face another garden: the West Garden that survives intact to this day, the Xu Garden.

The history of the Xu Garden goes back to the Ming dynasty. After Zhu Di took the throne he made his second son Zhu Gaoxu the Prince of Han, and this place became the Prince of Han's residence. Later generations took the character xu from his name, and that is how the garden got its name.

When the Kangxi and Qianlong emperors toured the south they repeatedly stayed at the Jiangning Weaving Office and the garden of the Viceroy of Liangjiang, both of which refer to this same place.

When the Xiang army broke into Nanjing, the Heavenly Court Palace of the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom, having poor fire protection, burned for several days and was badly damaged. Under Zeng Guofan the Xu Garden was rebuilt, and what we see today is the appearance after that reconstruction.

![The Unmoored Boat moored at the pond edge, its white stone hull carrying a grey-tiled pavilion and joined to the bank by a bridge.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/7447284de8bf769cf9fd6f4d0c94660e.webp)

During the Qianlong reign the Viceroy of Liangjiang carried out major works on this garden. When Qianlong toured the south he bestowed a name on this stone boat in the garden, the Unmoored Boat, which became the garden's greatest treasure.

The stone boat is a fairly common form in ancient gardens: the Summer Palace has the Qingyan Boat and the Humble Administrator's Garden has Xiangzhou, and this Unmoored Boat is another representative of the genre. In imperial and government gardens the image chiefly carries the meaning that water can float a boat and also capsize it, symbolising a realm secure for ever and peace across the land. For reclusive literati it means more the sense in the Lie Yukou chapter of Zhuangzi: the clever labour and the wise fret, while the incapable have nothing to seek, eat their fill and wander, drifting like an unmoored boat — a free and easy detachment, at home wherever they are.

![The Xu Garden's long narrow lake mirrors pavilions on both banks, with Xijia Tower on the left and a small arched bridge beyond.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/3c127f1e9ef28ff58f02521021d8177e.webp)

At the heart of the Xu Garden is a long, narrow lake, ringed by pavilions and terraces.

On the left of the photo is Xijia Tower, the best place in the garden to watch the water. Along the water on its ground floor runs a row of Wu King's backrests — the kind of seat with a low railing — which are found in many modern parks in Jiangnan cities. The form is named after King Fuchai of Wu, who built a palace for his frail favourite Xi Shi, and whose craftsmen designed this waterside backrest for leaning and resting; later literati derived from it the name beauty's backrest.

![Two heavily weathered steles stand side by side against a white wall under a timber porch, their inscriptions worn nearly illegible.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/3a68df706080649ed6f00fb282f6760e.webp)

West of Xijia Tower stand two steles whose inscriptions are hard to make out, though they have been framed by buildings. These are two steles in the Qianlong emperor's own hand, roughly written to commend and to urge on two of the viceroys of Liangjiang.

![Cream-yellow Republican-era arcade with white moulded arches and carved capitals, a grey-tiled corridor glimpsed through the opening.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/ccc4bbed1fe3fa30246d5edad2c2e297.webp)

We did not tour the eastern shore of the Xu Garden; passing through a doorway we were back in the Republican world. Several buildings here hold information-dense history galleries. We read a little of the beginning, which covered several uprisings of the late Qing, and realised this was wrong — there was no time today for that kind of visit — so we hurried back out.

After the 1911 Revolution Sun Yat-sen's provisional presidential office was set up in this area. The office building was formerly the West Flower Hall built by the Viceroy of Liangjiang in the late Qing, the only single-storey Western-style building in the viceroy's compound, used to serve foreign guests and senior staff.

When the Nationalist government needed to find office space for Sun Yat-sen, conventional thinking would have put it up around the Zichao Building at the north end of the central route. But the Zichao Building did not exist then, and the north end of the central route was the inner residential quarter of the Liangjiang yamen, dense and narrow, unable to meet the efficiency needs of a large administrative staff. With troubles at home and threats abroad, there was also no time to wait for premises. The open West Flower Hall, by contrast, could be put into use with simple alterations.

Work on the Zichao Building began in 1934. By then Sun Yat-sen had been dead for nine years, the Nanjing Nationalist government's establishment had outgrown what this West Flower Hall could hold, and demolishing the inner residential quarter of the yamen to rebuild a modern office block followed naturally.

![A long green-clothed meeting table faces a wall mural of Sun Yat-sen's inauguration, three flags standing behind the assembled figures.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/70b1bd7ada16f0a21d679c8bd861235a.webp)

A large meeting hall in the provisional presidential office; note the three flags in the picture.

In the early days of the 1911 Revolution, when the southern provinces declared independence, there was no unified flag. The Wuchang Uprising flew the Iron-Blood Eighteen-Star Flag, the one on the right, representing the eighteen provinces where Han Chinese lived inside the passes, in line with Sun Yat-sen's original principle of expelling the Manchus and restoring China. The Revolutionary Alliance and the Guangdong revolutionaries flew the Blue Sky White Sun flag, the one on the left, representing freedom and equality. The Jiangsu-Zhejiang allied army and the constitutionalists flew the Five-Colored Flag, standing for the five peoples — Han, Manchu, Mongol, Hui and Tibetan — and symbolising the republic of the five peoples and a world held in common.

When Sun Yat-sen took office as provisional president in Nanjing a vote was held on the national flag, and the Five-Colored Flag won by an overwhelming majority. The Eighteen-Star Flag and the Blue Sky White Sun flag were designated the army flag and the navy flag respectively.

![The Five-Colored Flag hung on a white wall, its bands running red, yellow, blue, white and black from top to bottom.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/54907397d3dcd0e24c594ac05093907c.webp)

On the wall on the other side of the meeting room hangs a huge Five-Colored Flag, its stripes standing in order for Han, Manchu, Mongol, Hui and Tibetan.

When the Qing emperor abdicated, the abdication edict was repeatedly weighed and polished by all sides. The constitutionalist leader Zhang Jian, from Jiangsu-Zhejiang, was the chief author and wrote into it that the complete territories of the five peoples — Manchu, Mongol, Han, Hui and Tibetan — would remain joined as one great Republic of China. This is a very subtle point: had the edict been read as the Qing empire being overthrown by the Han, the powers could immediately and openly have incited independence in the minority regions. In law this edict preserved the territory under Qing rule and also set the goal for later reunification. And of course it helped bring this flag about.

Sun Yat-sen himself, however, did not accept the Five-Colored Flag and reserved his opinion. He held that the design came from the pennant of a first-rank admiral of the Qing Beiyang fleet, that it did not cut cleanly enough with the old forces, and that the vertical arrangement was bad for expressing equality among the peoples.

Long before the 1911 Revolution, Sun Yat-sen had discussed flags with revolutionaries of all sides, and several designs were deadlocked. Sun favoured the Blue Sky White Sun flag, which Huang Xing firmly opposed as too close to Japan's Rising Sun flag, while the Iron-Blood Eighteen-Star and the Five-Colored Flag each had supporters. Sun proposed a compromise: shrink the Blue Sky White Sun to a quarter and place it in the upper left corner, fill the rest with red for the blood shed in revolution. That became the fourth design, the flag of Blue Sky, White Sun and a Wholly Red Earth.

After the south broke with Yuan Shikai, the north kept using the Five-Colored Flag. Sun Yat-sen began to re-establish revolutionary legitimacy and adopted the Blue Sky, White Sun and a Wholly Red Earth flag in Guangzhou. With the second Northern Expedition and Zhang Xueliang's Northeast Flag Replacement, the Fengtian clique's Five-Colored Flag was also changed to the Blue Sky, White Sun and a Wholly Red Earth flag, and the Five-Colored Flag left the stage of history for good.

Inside the memorial hall of the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum, if you look up at the ceiling, there is a huge Blue Sky White Sun flag.

![Visitors step through the stone doorway of a grey brick building whose lintel reads Sun Yat-sen and the Nanjing Provisional Government.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/fd32c00d737f62e552293c143bc8ef57.webp)

In another building north of the presidential office there is an exhibition on Sun Yat-sen and the Nanjing provisional government.

![Wax figures recreate the inauguration, Sun Yat-sen in a pale Zhongshan suit at centre beside Huang Xing in earth-yellow uniform.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/12d41ff8e50a6f327c8f874ba0ebd176.webp)

Just inside the entrance is a wax-figure area recreating Sun Yat-sen swearing the oath as provisional president. The man beside him in earth-yellow uniform is Huang Xing, the military commander and executive core of the revolution. There are also Cai Yuanpei, Song Jiaoren, Zhang Jian and other figures mentioned earlier — the Revolutionary Alliance's young guard, old constitutionalist gentry, officers of the uprising's new armies and overseas advisers, all gathered in one room.

Further in there are large numbers of text panels, which we did not examine; we came back out and carried on.

![The Yilan Pavilion's red lattice doors face the lotus-covered pond across the water, framed by two slender Taihu stones.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/f4685c0395d43663c33adbcda320e417.webp)

Passing the Guandi Temple we reached the northern shore of the Xu Garden. To the south are the Yilan Pavilion and a corner of the pond.

![The dark timber veranda of Sun Yat-sen's living quarters, its open glazed doors revealing a portrait and a vase inside.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/819e8086380bbbc8e71752e7094054e6.webp)

Beside it is a quiet little courtyard, Sun Yat-sen's living quarters. More than a month after Sun Yat-sen began his life here as provisional president, his wife and daughter came to join him, and they lived together here for a short period until Sun stepped down in favour of Yuan Shikai.

How frugally the first family of the day lived here — you have to come and see it to feel it.

![Two carved stone fish tanks stand in the courtyard, each enclosed in a black iron cage.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/713933521e4a260de119f23d64a9ea69.webp)

Two Western fish tanks stand in the courtyard, gifts to Sun Yat-sen from overseas Chinese. Sun once remarked that overseas Chinese were the mother of the revolution.

That was the end of the Presidential Palace; we left the site and hurried to catch the train home.

## Local Life

### Geography

Nanjing lies in the inland west of Jiangsu, basically next to Anhui. As a provincial capital its transport network is very developed, with many high-speed rail services from the surrounding provinces.

Nanjing sits right beside the Yangtze, with its core urban districts on the southeast bank.

By early November Nanjing already feels the chill of late autumn, with temperatures roughly in the 10-20 range; three layers is the mainstream way to dress.

We ran into rain on this trip, though only a little on the third day at the Presidential Palace, so it did not really affect getting around.

### Hotels and Transport

Nanjing's sights are scattered across different parts of the city. For our zoo-plus-Republican-theme way of travelling, the most convenient location is around Daxinggong metro station, and that is where we booked our hotel.

![A Nanjing map with three arrows radiating from a circled city-centre point to Hongshan Forest Zoo, Xuanwu Lake and Zhongshan.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/7b6245076fe8a0141aefd8922c15260b.webp)

From Daxinggong station, Line 3 running north-south goes directly to Hongshan Forest Zoo and Xuanwu Lake, while Line 2 running east-west goes directly to the foot of Zhongshan; the Presidential Palace is a walk away.

### Food

Nanjing cooking belongs to the broader Huaiyang tradition, and you can find lion's head meatballs and blanched shredded dried tofu here too.

![A bowl of blanched shredded dried tofu heaped in dark soy dressing, topped with diced carrot and celery.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/2ccfdd5e0b19bc3c9ffc1accb36049b4.webp)

Nanjing Dapaidang's blanched shredded dried tofu.

![Sliced Jinling roast duck with glossy mahogany skin and sesame seeds, resting in a pool of dark sauce.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/a84216c89f07ccd28a38755915f6f2f9.webp)

Jinling roast duck. Nanjing's speciality is duck, and you have surely heard the saying that no duck can swim out of Nanjing alive. Beijing roast duck may be more famous, but historically it came from Nanjing, carried north when the Yongle emperor moved the capital. Nanjing's roast duck is served like this, with a sauce.

![Cream-coloured Meiling porridge, fine rice grains suspended in soy milk, with a porcelain spoon resting in the bowl.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/cc9bcab3bd895dba013a759c079d6d69.webp)

Meiling porridge, a name attached by later generations, with no provable connection to Soong Mei-ling. Celebrities are always being made to endorse things.

It is made by cooking rice and glutinous rice in fresh soy milk instead of water, with some yam paste mixed in. A very good dessert. I had Nanjing Dapaidang in Hangzhou too, and the Meiling porridge there tastes different — the soy aroma is not as strong as this.

![Sizzling eel in a black clay pot, dark glossy strips in thick sauce scattered with black pepper and shredded scallion.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/b81388f4c8e8455ccbf2fb501aa8f81a.webp)

Sizzling eel. The dish exists across Jiangsu, Zhejiang and Shanghai, but its origin is in Jiangsu. Heavily thickened with starch, salty and sweet at once. This restaurant's version used a lot of black pepper, which was a surprise, but it was strangely good.

![Two pingtan musicians perform beneath rows of warm paper lanterns in the dining hall, a Qing portrait behind them.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/213e6e73f8fd379c6bf06909bf4cfffc.webp)

Why eat at Nanjing Dapaidang when it is all over major Chinese cities and we were already in Nanjing? Because this branch has Suzhou pingtan and Jiangnan folk tunes, and the atmosphere is wonderful. I did not know in advance; we were eating when the stage began to sing. This is the branch near Muxuyuan metro station.

At midday on the Presidential Palace day we ate at Nanjing Dapaidang in the 1912 Block again. We always do this — when travelling, once we like a restaurant we go back repeatedly rather than risk a bad meal elsewhere.

![Sliced salted duck with pale skin and moist flesh, plated beside a handful of boiled peanuts.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/4012c58d635004ea247ea92a35b44999.webp)

Salted duck. Well-made salted duck does not taste salty at all, yet the savoury flavour comes through completely.

![Braised pork and glutinous rice in a bamboo steamer, glossy soy-stained grains capping pork belly slices over a lotus leaf.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/862ddd7f94fc6a9ffe97b86a7b2a2bdb.webp)

Braised pork with glutinous rice, ordered for our daughter, who loves it.

![Shredded dried tofu simmered in milky broth in an earthenware pot, with slivers of ham floating on the surface.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/a66602c1b626d2b7a7cae01b8e3cb3c7.webp)

Another way with shredded dried tofu: cooked in broth. Jiangnan cooking often puts ham into the broth, and the savoury flavour released by fermentation is the soul of the soup.

![Three roast duck buns in a bamboo steamer, their pleated tops open to reveal the broth inside.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/e2c8b1ca17af8804a8d28bf5d3b62450.webp)

Roast duck bun. Yes, the filling is duck.

![Pingtan musicians perform on a balcony stage above the dining tables in a two-storey atrium hung with paper lanterns.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/da56e4d319f8facbc0337bf3c1eac6d6.webp)

If you get the chance you must come to this Nanjing Dapaidang in the 1912 Block. Indoors it is a two-storey building with the atrium open above and below, pingtan sung in the small side rooms, and looking straight across the room it has exactly that classical feel.

The other meals were more casual and not particularly distinctive, so I will not bring them out here.

### Observations

![Looking up through the canopy of plane trees to a clear blue sky, the tops of office towers framed between the branches.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/4ef54272f44ef38d20619720b68a3738.webp)

In Nanjing it is hard not to notice the plane trees — also called Platanus — lining the streets. When the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum was completed, in preparation for the grand funeral of Mr Sun Yat-sen, the Nanjing municipal authorities introduced around 20,000 plane trees. Plane trees grow fast and cast wide shade, and quickly established the city's distinctive look.

In the 1950s Liu Bocheng, Nanjing's first mayor, admired the height and shade of the plane trees and proposed planting them across the city. After this second large-scale planting, plane trees spread through almost every street and alley of Nanjing and became the city's signature.

![A modern residential block with tiers of balustraded balconies and a green-tiled Chinese eave at street level, plane trees beside it.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/e300e7f831f899a685012b557410de6c.webp)

Plane trees on one side, modern housing with Republican-era architectural touches on the other.

![A black enamel house-number plate with white characters giving the street name and number 52, fixed to a grey rendered wall.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/9fa8b121be5203ae83eaaaab34bdc819.webp)

In some corners of the city, period house-number plates have been deliberately preserved.

![A bronze circular relief on a metro station wall carved with the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum gate, a child in pink reaching up to touch it.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/eee4fac41a1f49726700fd8d3b8cbb9f.webp)

As a capital of six dynasties, Nanjing has far too many stories to tell. City-specific cultural symbols are woven into public space: this is an installation in a metro station, carved with the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum.

![A red-lacquered cabinet in the hotel room, its black doors painted with gold-outlined floral scrollwork and brass ring pulls.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/d645698444797c577b20f01ffb062824.webp)

Even the hotel we stayed in had cabinets of this shape.

![Two vintage toggle switches with wooden faceplates and knurled brass levers, mounted beside a modern socket.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/39c1e0c83570faae6217677ebb1daf05.webp)

In the 1912 Block we ate at a restaurant with a nicely atmospheric interior that still kept this old-style light switch.

![A framed old map of Nanjing with Xuanwu Lake across the top and the Ming Palace airfield marked in the lower right.](https://cdn.victor42.work/posts/2026-11/e3848603646f1ae53ff79d96efc0f82e.webp)

Inside there is also an old map of Nanjing. It turns out there used to be an airfield near the Ming Palace ruins, and from the map you can see that Xuanwu Lake already looked much as it does today.
